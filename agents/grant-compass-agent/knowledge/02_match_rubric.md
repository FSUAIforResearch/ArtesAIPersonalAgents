# Research-to-Opportunity Match Rubric (`02_match_rubric.md`)

Use this rubric to evaluate alignment between a researcher's project concept and a specific funding opportunity. Never output numerical match percentages, fundability scores, reviewer score predictions, or award probabilities.

---

## 1. Six Evaluation Dimensions

| Dimension | What to Compare | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Program Purpose & Priority Alignment** | Sponsor's stated mission, funding priorities, eligible topics, target populations, geographic focus, and intended outcomes vs. the project's aims. | Distinguishes genuine substantive alignment from surface keyword overlap. |
| **2. Apparent Eligibility & Application Limits** | Eligible institution types, PI appointment/degree/career-stage rules, prior-award restrictions, required registrations, and institutional/PI application caps. | Identifies hard eligibility or limited-submission blockers before drafting begins. |
| **3. Mechanism, Budget, & Duration Fit** | Mechanism type (pilot, R01-style research, career award, training, equipment, center), award floor/ceiling, direct vs. total-cost basis, and project period vs. project stage and budget estimate. | A project can match the topic but be too large, too early-stage, or too clinical/definitive for the mechanism. |
| **4. Team, Collaborator, & Partnership Fit** | Required multi-disciplinary expertise, community organizations, clinical sites, school districts, industry partners, mentors, or subawards vs. confirmed team commitments. | Expressions of interest are **not** confirmed partnerships or signed commitment letters. |
| **5. Review-Criteria & Evidence Readiness** | Published evaluation criteria and weights vs. the researcher's available preliminary data, methods detail, evaluation plan, and dissemination strategy. | Highlights what evidence exists now versus what must be developed during proposal writing. |
| **6. Timeline & Submission Feasibility** | Time remaining before LOI/preapplication/full deadline, FSU limited-submission selection, partner letters, subrecipient packages, and RAMP routing. | Determines whether a strong scientific match is realistically achievable in the current cycle. |

---

## 2. Qualitative Fit Classifications (Choose One)

| Classification | When to Assign |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Strong apparent fit (`Strong Match`)** | The project substantially aligns with the central program purpose and priorities; mechanism, project stage, budget, and duration are compatible; material eligibility facts are met; and no submission-blocking mismatch is identified. |
| **Possible fit (`Possible Match`)** | Meaningful research alignment exists, but material questions remain unresolved—such as an unconfirmed required partner letter, an institutional limited-submission selection process, budget/scope resizing, or missing eligibility details. |
| **Weak apparent fit (`Weak Match`)** | The project conflicts with the program's core scope or eligible activities (e.g., proposing a definitive efficacy trial for a pilot-only solicitation), fails a verified eligibility rule, or relies only on shared keywords. |
| **Provisional — More Information Needed** | Promising topical match, but decisive official solicitation guidelines, review criteria, or core project facts could not yet be accessed or verified. |

### Classification Rules & Required Disclaimer
- **Eligibility Conflicts**: A verified eligibility mismatch results in **Weak apparent fit** (never treat a hard eligibility conflict as a minor framing gap).
- **Framing vs. Substantive Gaps**:
  - **Framing Opportunity**: Making existing, accurate alignment clearer using the sponsor's structure and terminology (never misrepresenting aims, methods, or preliminary results).
  - **Substantive Project Gap**: Missing elements that require real changes to project design, scope, budget, partnerships, or preliminary data.
- **Mandatory Caution**: Include this note with every fit classification:
  > *This classification describes alignment with the available opportunity and project information. It is not a funding prediction or final eligibility determination.*

---

## 3. Action Decision Categories (Choose One)

End every detailed project-to-grant match (`/match-opportunity`) with one of these four recommendations and a 1–2 sentence rationale:

1. **Pursue**: Strong alignment and feasible timeline; proceed to proposal planning (`/plan-proposal`) and outlining (`/outline-proposal`).
2. **Investigate Further**: Promising fit (`Possible Match`), but 1–2 specific blockers (such as FSU limited-submission clearance, partner commitment, or program officer inquiry) must be resolved before committing full writing effort.
3. **Monitor**: Good thematic fit, but the current deadline is too close, closed, or requires preliminary data/partnerships best prepared for the next cycle.
4. **Do Not Pursue**: Verified eligibility conflict, out-of-scope mechanism, or fundamental mismatch; pivot to alternative sponsors (`/find-funding`).
