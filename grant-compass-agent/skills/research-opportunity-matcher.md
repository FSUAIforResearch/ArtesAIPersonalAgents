# Research Opportunity Matcher (`/match-opportunity`)

## When to Apply This Skill

Apply when the user types `/match-opportunity` or asks whether their research project, concept note, or specific aims fit a particular grant solicitation. Follow the workflow below and end with a clear decision recommendation and three prioritized next actions.

## Objective

Compare a faculty member's non-confidential research concept against a specific funding opportunity to produce a transparent, qualitative fit assessment. Distinguish alignment that can be clarified through better **framing** from **substantive project gaps** that require real design, partner, or capability changes—without ever recommending that the researcher misrepresent their aims, data, or readiness.

## Required Inputs & Data Minimization

Require only:
1. **A short, non-confidential project description** (concept summary, aims, methods, target population/setting, approximate budget/duration, and current readiness).
2. **A specific grant opportunity** (official URL, uploaded solicitation, or opportunity brief from earlier in the conversation).

Do not require raw research data, participant records, PHI, student records, or a complete proposal draft. Ask at most 1–3 follow-up questions only if a missing fact (such as career stage on an early-career mechanism or partner availability on a partnership-mandated grant) materially changes the conclusion.

## Step-by-Step Matching Workflow

### 1. Check Submission Blockers & Eligibility First
Evaluate known project and investigator facts against explicit sponsor rules:
- Closed status or missed mandatory LOI/preapplication.
- Institutional or PI eligibility conflicts (institution type, appointment, career stage).
- Institutional application limits (Limited Submission requiring FSU Research Development clearance).
- Mandatory cost sharing or required community/industry/multi-institutional partners.
Do not classify a verified eligibility conflict as a minor gap.

### 2. Evaluate Substantive Alignment Across Six Dimensions
Compare the project against the official grant guidelines (never relying on keyword overlap alone):
- **Program Purpose & Priorities**: Does the research directly address named program goals and target populations/settings?
- **Mechanism & Scope Fit**: Does the project stage (e.g., exploratory pilot vs. full efficacy trial), budget estimate, and duration fit the mechanism's ceiling and allowed activities?
- **Methods & Evidence Readiness**: Are the proposed methods permitted, and does the project meet any stated preliminary-data expectations?
- **Team, Collaborator, & Capability Needs**: Does the project have (or need to add) required disciplinary collaborators, community partners, evaluators, or facilities?
- **Review-Criteria Alignment**: How well can the supplied concept address each published review criterion?
- **Timeline & FSU Feasibility**: Is there realistic time for partner letters, RAMP routing, and internal review before the deadline?

### 3. Separate Framing Opportunities from Substantive Project Gaps
- **Framing Opportunities**: Ways to communicate existing, accurate alignment more clearly for the sponsor's review criteria.
- **Substantive Project Gaps**: Missing elements that require real changes to scope, budget, methods, partnerships, or preliminary evidence.
- **Research Integrity Rule**: Never advise a researcher to exaggerate preliminary findings, invent partnerships, or misrepresent methods or capabilities to fit a solicitation.

### 4. Assign Qualitative Fit & Decision Classifications
Never output a numerical match percentage, fundability score, reviewer score prediction, or award probability.
1. **Apparent Fit Category** (choose one):
   - **Strong apparent fit (`Strong Match`)**: Substantive alignment across program purpose, mechanism, budget, and eligibility with no explicit blocker.
   - **Possible fit (`Possible Match`)**: Meaningful alignment, but important issues (e.g., unconfirmed required partner, limited-submission selection, scope resizing, or missing details) must be resolved.
   - **Weak apparent fit (`Weak Match`)**: Conflicts with core program scope/eligibility or relies only on surface keyword overlap.
   - **Provisional — More Information Needed**: Critical solicitation requirements or project facts are unavailable (e.g., blocked site requiring solicitation PDF upload).
2. **Action Decision** (choose one and explain briefly):
   - **Pursue** · **Investigate Further** · **Monitor (for future cycle)** · **Do Not Pursue**

## Required Output Structure

1. **Submission-Blocking Issues & Eligibility Risks** (listed first)
2. **Overall Fit Classification & Action Decision** (`Strong apparent fit` / `Possible fit` / `Weak apparent fit` / `Provisional` + `Pursue` / `Investigate Further` / `Monitor` / `Do Not Pursue`, with the disclaimer that this is an alignment assessment, not a funding prediction or final eligibility determination)
3. **Dimension-by-Dimension Alignment Summary** (Program Alignment, Mechanism/Budget/Duration Fit, Evidence Readiness, Team/Partnership Needs, and Review-Criteria Fit)
4. **Framing Opportunities vs. Substantive Project Gaps**
5. **FSU Considerations** (include only when limited submissions, routing to SRA/FSURF, or compliance referrals materially apply)
6. **Recommended Next Three Actions** (and official sources used)
