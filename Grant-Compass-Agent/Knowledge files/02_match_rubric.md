# Grant Compass Research-to-Opportunity Match Rubric

## Purpose

Use this rubric when comparing a faculty research concept with a verified funding opportunity. The rubric supports a transparent qualitative assessment. It does not determine final eligibility, institutional approval, competitiveness, fundability, or probability of award.

## Prerequisites

Do not assess fit until the available opportunity information has been verified against the current official sponsor source.

At minimum, attempt to confirm:

- Sponsor and program title.
- Opportunity number.
- Current status.
- Deadline, time, and time zone.
- Letter of intent or preapplication requirement.
- Institution and investigator eligibility.
- Institutional or investigator application limit.
- Award amount or expected range.
- Project period.
- Purpose, priorities, and eligible activities.
- Required partners, populations, settings, methods, or outcomes.
- Review criteria.
- Material restrictions, cost sharing, and submission requirements.

If key facts are unavailable, continue only with a provisional assessment and identify the missing facts.

## Required faculty/project inputs

Collect only information material to the current match:

- Department and discipline.
- Research topic and short abstract.
- Research objective or aims.
- Proposed methods or approach.
- Population, setting, or geographic focus.
- Project stage.
- Approximate funding need.
- Expected duration.
- Collaborators or required partners.
- Target submission period.
- Career-stage facts when relevant.
- Human-participant, vertebrate-animal, international, controlled-data, or sensitive-data flags.
- Preferred or excluded sponsors.

Do not request citizenship, residency, protected information, unpublished patentable details, export-controlled information, or other sensitive facts unless a specific opportunity makes the information necessary and the environment is approved to process it.

## Match dimensions

### 1. Program alignment

Evaluate whether the project corresponds with the sponsor's stated:

- Purpose.
- Funding priorities.
- Research topics.
- Eligible activities.
- Target populations.
- Settings or geography.
- Intended outcomes.

Possible findings:

- Strong alignment with central program purpose.
- Partial alignment requiring reframing or narrower aims.
- Superficial keyword overlap but weak substantive alignment.
- Explicitly outside the program scope.

### 2. Eligibility

Evaluate only explicit, verified eligibility conditions, including:

- Eligible institution type.
- Principal investigator or project director requirements.
- Career-stage restrictions.
- Citizenship or residency only when explicitly required.
- Geographic restrictions.
- Prior-award or resubmission restrictions.
- Required registrations or memberships.
- Institutional or investigator application limits.

Use language such as:

- Apparent eligibility based on the supplied facts.
- Eligibility requires verification.
- Potential eligibility conflict.
- Explicit eligibility mismatch.

Never state final eligibility unless an authoritative sponsor or institutional process has confirmed it.

### 3. Mechanism fit

Compare:

- Project stage with mechanism purpose.
- Requested budget with award range or ceiling.
- Proposed duration with allowed project period.
- Research, training, fellowship, equipment, center, conference, or planning mechanism.
- Required deliverables.
- Preliminary-data or readiness expectations.

### 4. Team and partnership fit

Evaluate whether the opportunity requires or strongly favors:

- Multiple disciplines.
- Community partners.
- School districts.
- Clinical sites.
- Industry partners.
- Minority-serving institutions or specific institutional partners.
- Multi-institutional teams.
- Subawards or consortia.
- Mentors, advisory boards, evaluators, or trainees.

Do not invent collaborators or commitments.

### 5. Review-criteria fit

Compare the concept with published review criteria. Identify:

- Criteria the concept appears positioned to address.
- Criteria that require additional evidence or planning.
- Missing preliminary evidence, evaluation detail, impact pathway, dissemination, management, or broader-impacts content.

Do not assume that alignment with review criteria predicts funding success.

### 6. Feasibility and blockers

Check for:

- Deadline feasibility.
- Letter of intent or preapplication deadline.
- Limited submission.
- Mandatory cost sharing.
- Cost sharing prohibited.
- Required partner or site.
- Geographic restrictions.
- Required preliminary results.
- Data-access constraints.
- Human-subject, animal, biosafety, export-control, research-security, or sensitive-data considerations.
- Budget or project-period mismatch.
- Submission-system or registration requirements.

## Fit classifications

### Strong apparent fit

Use only when:

- The concept substantially aligns with the central program purpose and priorities.
- The project stage, budget, duration, and mechanism are broadly compatible.
- No explicit blocking mismatch has been identified.
- Material eligibility facts are verified or clearly identified as pending.
- The project appears capable of addressing the published review criteria.

Required caution:

> Strong apparent fit describes alignment with the available opportunity information. It is not a funding prediction or final eligibility determination.

### Possible fit

Use when:

- Meaningful alignment exists, but one or more important issues require verification or adjustment.
- The concept may need reframing, a different aim, an added partner, a reduced budget, a different timeline, or additional evidence.
- Eligibility or scope language is ambiguous.
- A limited submission, LOI, cost-share requirement, or other blocker may apply.

### Weak apparent fit

Use when:

- The concept conflicts with the central program purpose or eligible activities.
- A verified eligibility rule appears not to be met.
- The project stage, budget, duration, population, geography, required partnership, or mechanism is materially incompatible.
- Alignment depends mainly on shared keywords rather than substantive program fit.

When fit is weak, explain the mismatch and suggest a better search direction. Do not simply reject the idea.

## Prohibited outputs

Do not provide:

- Match percentages.
- Probability of award.
- Fundability scores.
- Predicted reviewer scores.
- Guaranteed eligibility.
- Guaranteed competitiveness.
- Final compliance conclusions.
- Claims that the proposal will or will not be funded.

## Required output structure

### Apparent Fit

State one classification:

- Strong apparent fit
- Possible fit
- Weak apparent fit

### Alignment

List the most important evidence-based points of correspondence.

### Potential Mismatches

List scope, mechanism, budget, duration, partner, population, method, or readiness concerns.

### Eligibility Concerns

Identify verified conditions and conditions still requiring confirmation.

### Review-Criteria Fit

Explain which criteria appear addressable and which require development.

### Submission Blockers

Identify deadlines, LOIs, institutional limits, cost share, required partners, or other blocking issues.

### Missing Information

List only missing information that materially affects the assessment.

### Recommended Verification

Identify the exact sponsor source, FSU office, or question that should be checked next.

### Recommended Next Three Actions

Provide three concrete actions in priority order.

## Decision rules

- A verified eligibility mismatch normally results in Weak apparent fit unless an authoritative exception or alternative applicant structure exists.
- An unresolved eligibility fact normally prevents Strong apparent fit.
- A budget mismatch alone may result in Possible fit if the project can be credibly resized.
- A missed mandatory LOI or closed deadline is a submission blocker and should appear before the fit discussion.
- Limited-submission status does not determine scientific fit, but it can prevent independent submission and must be surfaced prominently.
- Keyword overlap is not sufficient evidence of fit.
- Do not penalize a concept for missing proposal-writing detail that would normally be developed after an opportunity is selected. Distinguish concept mismatch from undeveloped application content.

## Quality check

Before returning the assessment, verify that:

- Every fit claim is tied to the sponsor's stated purpose, scope, eligibility, or review criteria.
- No missing fact was converted into a confident conclusion.
- No percentage or award prediction was used.
- Submission blockers appear near the beginning.
- The final actions are specific and feasible.
