# Highly specific test case

## Test Prompt 1 - HeatReady Florida: Protecting Older Adults from Extreme Heat in Rural Communities.

I’m an assistant professor at Florida State University studying community resilience and environmental health. I’m looking for funding for a project called **HeatReady Florida: Protecting Older Adults from Extreme Heat in Rural Communities**.

My team wants to investigate how housing conditions, social isolation, and access to transportation affect heat exposure among adults aged 65 and older in rural North Florida. We also want to test whether a community-designed heat preparedness program can reduce exposure and improve protective behaviors.

We propose three aims:

1. Identify household and neighborhood factors associated with elevated indoor heat exposure.
2. Develop a heat preparedness program with older residents and local community organizations.
3. Conduct a small pilot study to assess the program’s feasibility, acceptability, and preliminary effects.

We anticipate recruiting approximately 120 older adults across two rural counties. Participants would complete surveys, and a subset would receive indoor temperature and humidity sensors. We would also interview residents and community service providers. The pilot program could include personalized heat action plans, telephone check-ins, and information about local cooling resources.

Our team includes researchers with expertise in public health, geography, and social work. Two community organizations have expressed interest, but we do not yet have formal partnership agreements. We have conducted 12 exploratory interviews but have not collected sensor data or tested the intervention.

We estimate needing **$450,000 over three years**, although we can adjust the scope. Funding would support a graduate research assistant, participant compensation, sensors, travel, community partner participation, and data analysis.

The intended outputs are a preliminary dataset, a community-informed intervention, academic publications, and a practical toolkit for rural service providers. We hope this project will position us for a larger intervention study.

We are open to federal agencies, foundations, and relevant pilot funding. Ideally, we would submit an application within the next six months.

Which funding opportunities should we consider, and what additional information do you need to identify the strongest matches?

Best prospects to investigate
1. FSU Spring 2027 Seed Grant — best immediate fit
This program is intended to support preliminary work that can strengthen an external funding application, and it encourages multidisciplinary projects. Its Spring 2027 deadline is February 25, 2027, at 4:59 p.m.; awards average $50,000, with a maximum of $100,000. The award period begins June 14, 2027, and may last up to 24 months.  1  2
For HeatReady, consider framing a smaller, defined pilot—for example, co-designing and testing the preparedness program, refining recruitment and sensor procedures, and collecting feasibility data to support a later external proposal. Your proposed $450,000 over three years is well beyond this program’s cap, so this would not fund the full project. Seed funds do not support faculty salary; the program also limits total small-equipment purchases to $2,000, so confirm how the proposed sensors would be classified and whether community-partner costs are allowable.  1

Additional information that would sharpen the match
The most useful details are:

Target submission window: Are you aiming for a full external proposal by May 2027, or would an FSU seed award first be useful?
Pilot design: Will participants be randomized or assigned to a comparison group? What are the primary outcomes, and when will you measure them? This helps identify which sponsor mechanisms could accept the study design.
Heat-exposure and health measures: What will sensors measure, for how long, and what health or protective-behavior outcomes will you collect?
Geography and recruitment: Which two counties, how will you recruit participants, and what recruitment or service-provider commitments can partners make?
Partner roles and budget: Would the organizations receive subawards, contracts, or other support? What costs are essential, and which can be scaled down?
Career-stage and funding history: When did you begin your faculty appointment, and what relevant grants or FSU CRC awards have you or proposed co-investigators received? Those details can affect early-career mechanisms and internal-program eligibility.
Human-subjects status: Have the 12 exploratory interviews been reviewed or discussed with FSU’s Office for Human Subjects Protection? Because the planned work includes interviews, surveys, and an intervention pilot, OHSP/IRB guidance may apply; FSU says required IRB approval must come before covered human-subject activity begins.
