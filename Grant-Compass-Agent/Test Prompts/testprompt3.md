# Test Prompt 3
I’m at FSU and have an idea for a research project involving technology and education. Can you help me find funding? I haven’t worked out the details yet.

## Response 1

Absolutely. “Technology and education” is a useful starting point, and you don’t need a polished project description yet. A few rough notes will help me find funding that’s genuinely relevant rather than just keyword matches.

What technology or approach are you considering—for example, AI, educational software, learning analytics, assistive technology, or online learning?
Who and what setting would the project focus on—such as K–12 students, college students, teachers, adult learners, or a particular community—and what would you hope to improve?
What kind of support and timing are you imagining: early-stage or pilot research, a larger study, equipment or training, and a particular submission window?

You can answer in fragments or say “not sure” for any of these. Please avoid sharing confidential, sensitive, or unpublished details.

## Prompt 2

1. I’m interested in AI, possibly a tutoring tool that gives students feedback while they work through science problems. I haven’t decided whether to adapt an existing tool or develop something new.

2. The focus would be undergraduate students in introductory science courses at FSU. I’d like to improve their understanding and confidence, potentially with a focus on first-generation college students.

3. I’m looking for early-stage or pilot funding to refine the idea and run a small study. I’d like to apply within the next six months, but I’m not sure what budget or project duration would be realistic.

## Response 2

Best starting point: FSU Seed Grant
Your idea appears potentially well suited to FSU’s 2026–27 Seed Grant, especially if you frame it as a pilot study that generates preliminary evidence for a later external proposal, rather than as a project primarily to build instructional materials. The program is intended to support early research and expects the work to strengthen future external funding applications; it specifically says it will not fund proposals to develop instructional materials.  1
The next Seed deadline is February 25, 2027, at 4:59 p.m. The award averages $50,000, may not exceed $100,000, and can support a project period of up to 24 months. That gives you room to scope a small study, but the appropriate budget depends on choices such as adapting an existing tool versus developing a new one; the program asks applicants to justify the budget and feasibility.  1  2
Important eligibility check: Seed applicants must be full-time, active-status FSU faculty in an eligible job category; students and postdocs cannot apply as PI or Co-PI. The program also has restrictions related to prior Seed or AHPEG awards and limits applications within each competition round, so your appointment and recent CRC award history need checking.  1
Other promising directions to investigate


NSF Research on Innovative Technologies for Enhanced Learning (RITEL): The program description is a close topical match: it supports early-stage research involving emerging technologies such as AI in real-world learning settings, including STEM and all learner populations. It also emphasizes advancing research in both learning and technology, which favors a study of how the tutoring tool’s feedback affects learning—not only creating the tool. Unknown or unresolved: I could not verify a current submission window, deadline, or FSU limited-submission status, so treat RITEL as a lead, not a confirmed near-term application.  3


NSF Improving Undergraduate STEM Education (IUSE: EDU): This program supports work to improve undergraduate STEM teaching and learning, including research on what works and for whom. That makes it a reasonable direction if you develop a clear educational research question around learning and confidence. Unknown or unresolved: The available program page links to solicitation NSF 23-510, but I could not confirm a current-cycle deadline or FSU application-limit status.  4


I also checked the current IES deadlines: the FY 2027 “From Seedlings to Scale” application is due December 11, 2026, but its letter-of-intent deadline was October 29, 2026, and the available information does not establish a fit for this undergraduate-focused project. I would not prioritize it for this idea without further verification.  5
A practical way to shape the idea
A fundable pilot concept could focus on a specific question, such as whether a particular kind of AI-generated feedback helps students learn introductory science concepts or feel more confident solving problems. You do not need to decide immediately whether to build a new tool; comparing the feasibility of adapting an existing tool versus developing one could be an early planning decision. The Seed application will need to explain the defined work and how it could lead to external support.  1
Because the project may involve an intervention with FSU students and collection of learning or confidence data, FSU human-subjects review may apply. OHSP and the IRB determine the required review pathway, and review or approval must happen before covered study activities begin; consult OHSP while shaping the study rather than assuming a particular determination.  6
