# Prompt 2
I’m a researcher at Florida State University interested in how artificial intelligence could support students who struggle in introductory college science courses.

I’d like to explore whether an AI-based learning tool could help students understand difficult concepts and feel more confident in their coursework. I’m also interested in whether the tool benefits some groups of students more than others.

The project is still in an early stage, and I may collaborate with faculty from other disciplines. I’m looking for funding to help develop the idea and carry out an initial study.

Can you help me find suitable grants?

Best-fit opportunities

1. NSF Improving Undergraduate STEM Education: Directorate for STEM Education (IUSE: EDU) — strongest fit for the initial study
IUSE: EDU supports evidence-based, knowledge-generating projects to improve undergraduate STEM teaching and learning. Its solicitation specifically includes development and study of instructional tools and learning technologies, evaluation of student outcomes, and investigation of what works, for whom, and under what conditions—closely matching your goals. 1  2

Potential starting point: The Engaged Student Learning Level 1 track supports early-stage or exploratory work, with a maximum award of $400,000 and a maximum duration of three years. 1
Next deadline: The annual Level 1 deadline is the third Wednesday in January, at 5 p.m. the submitter’s local time; that date falls on January 20, 2027. 1
Eligibility and limits: The solicitation lists no limit on proposals per organization, but an individual may serve as PI or co-PI on no more than three IUSE: EDU proposals in a fiscal year. Voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited. 1
Important design implication: Frame this as a rigorous STEM-education study—not just a tool-development effort. Define learning and confidence outcomes, how you will examine differences in benefit, and how the study will generate evidence that can inform teaching beyond the pilot setting. 1  2

Apparent fit: Strong. The undergraduate STEM focus, exploratory project scale, and expectation to produce knowledge about student outcomes align especially well with your initial study. 1  2  This is an assessment of alignment, not a funding prediction or final eligibility determination.
2. NSF Research on Innovative Technologies for Enhanced Learning (RITEL) — strongest fit if the AI technology itself is a research contribution
RITEL supports early-stage research on emerging technologies—including AI—for teaching and learning in authentic educational settings. It explicitly encourages interdisciplinary work and requires projects to advance research in both learning and technology. 3
Your concept could fit if the proposal investigates not only whether students benefit, but also how the tool’s design or AI capabilities contribute to learning—for example, how it responds to student misconceptions or adapts explanations. A study that mainly evaluates an existing tool without a meaningful technology-research component may be a weaker match for RITEL’s distinctive emphasis. 3
