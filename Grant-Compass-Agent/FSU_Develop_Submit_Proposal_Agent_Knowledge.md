# FSU Sponsored Research Administration: Develop & Submit Proposal

> Source: https://www.research.fsu.edu/research-offices/sra/develop-submit-proposal/
>
> Purpose: AI-agent knowledge file capturing the content of the expandable sections on FSU Sponsored Research Administration's Develop & Submit Proposal page. Verify linked policies and current rates against the live FSU source before making compliance or budget decisions.

## Page overview

FSU Sponsored Research Administration (SRA) provides resources for developing, reviewing, and submitting sponsored proposals.

- For U.S. public funding (federal, state, or local government, including flow-through via private organizations), contact the department's Pre-Award Grant Officer.
- For private funding (nonprofit or for-profit) or foreign government/entity funding, contact the FSU Research Foundation.
- Proposals are submitted through RAMP, accessible through the myFSU Portal.

## Funding Resources

- FSU's funding opportunities page lists selected opportunities and is not exhaustive; faculty are encouraged to seek other sponsors as well.
- The Council on Research and Creativity (CRC) offers eight internal funding programs.
- Research Development (RD) provides access to Pivot, a searchable database with more than 28,000 private and public funding opportunities.
- The Office of Research publishes a newsletter containing funding announcements and research news.
- The FSU Foundation provides access to Foundation Center Online for private-foundation funding and can assist with managing gifts to the university.
- The Office of Faculty Development and Advancement assists with fellowships and award nominations.

## Limited Submissions

Some funding opportunities limit the number of proposals an institution may submit.

- Check FSU's limited-submissions listings to determine whether another PI has already claimed an opportunity.
- The listing is not comprehensive.
- If a limited-submission opportunity is not listed and you plan to apply, contact the Office of Research Development (ORD) immediately.

## Developing Your Proposal and Three Working Day Policy

- Consider reviewing examples of successfully funded proposals before writing the narrative. ORD provides FSU faculty access to a successful-proposal database.
- Departmental grants analysts can help develop budgets.
- For U.S. government funding, SRA departmental liaisons can advise; for private, nonprofit, or foreign-government funding, the FSU Research Foundation can advise.
- ORD maintains grant-writing resources and Research Development & Grant Writing News.
- The Office of Compliance provides a portal for research compliance issues.
- ORD staff can review narratives from a non-specialist perspective and may identify other faculty reviewers with relevant expertise.
- The Office of Faculty Development and Advancement can review fellowship or award applications and help locate references/referees.
- For limited submissions, consult FSU's Limited Submission website for the internal deadline.
- Once the budget, narrative, and other documents are drafted, the proposal is routed for departmental and SRA approval in RAMP.
- RAMP's Grants module Help Center includes guides for funding proposals and budgets. RAMP also supports system-to-system submission of Grants.gov proposals using the federal SF424 package.
- The PI is responsible for preparing all required proposal components and routing them to SRA through RAMP.
- **Three-working-day requirement:** a complete proposal must be department-approved and routed to SRA in RAMP by **9:00 AM at least three working days before the sponsor deadline**.
- SRA welcomes earlier notice and can review draft proposal documents before that deadline.

## Components of a Complete Proposal

A complete proposal for SRA review includes:

1. Funding Proposal SmartForm in RAMP-Grants.
2. Proposal Budget SmartForm in RAMP-Grants, with detailed budget justification attached to the Budget SmartForm.
3. Scope of Work. An accurate but rough technical version is acceptable at this stage; upload it to the Funding Proposal workspace.
4. Internal approval requests, when applicable, such as overmatch or subcontracting more than 75% of the award, with appropriate department, chair, and dean approvals documented as Ancillary Reviews in RAMP-Grants.
5. Subrecipient or Contractor Determination Form for entities receiving an outgoing subaward.
6. Commitment letters from entities receiving an outgoing subaward.
7. Sponsor RFP, solicitation, program announcement, or guidelines.
8. Other sponsor-required application documents, as applicable.
9. Evidence of conflict-of-interest training completion and financial-interest disclosures, as applicable. If subcontractors, consultants, or collaborators are designated by the PI as investigators, evidence of their training and disclosures is also required.

The final scope of work and updated proposal documents should reach SRA by **9:00 AM on the sponsor deadline** to allow time for unexpected submission issues.

## Budget Development

Budgets should be directly related to the proposed project. Federally funded project budgets must comply with **2 CFR 200 (Uniform Guidance)** and applicable Cost Accounting Standards (CAS).

### Salary

- List personnel employed by FSU; do not list non-FSU employees such as outside-organization personnel or consultants as FSU personnel.
- Identify each person's project role.
- Except for undergraduate students, budget personnel using percent effort or person-months rather than hourly rates.
- Undergraduate students may be budgeted hourly.
- Example: $80,000 annualized base salary at 10% FTE for a year = $8,000 project cost.
- Example: $80,000 annualized base salary for two summer months = $80,000 / 12 x 2 = $13,333.
- In RAMP, calculate Salary Requested percentage using months paid / months appointed. Example: two summer months for a 9-month appointment = 2/9 = 22%.
- For a budget period shorter than one year, first calculate the portion of the year covered. A six-month period is 50% of a year; for a 9-month appointment this corresponds to 4.5 adjusted appointment months. Two months worked / 4.5 = 44% salary for that six-month RAMP budget period.
- On federal projects, administrative/clerical salary is generally treated as indirect unless the services are integral to the project, the individuals are specifically identifiable with the project, the costs are explicitly budgeted and approved or prior written federal approval is received, and the costs are not also recovered as indirect costs.
- Explain in the budget justification why administrative/clerical positions are integral and ensure compliance with award terms.
- For multi-year budgets, the page recommends a 3% annual cost-of-living increase.

### Fringe Benefits

- Fringe benefits are calculated as a percentage of salary; consult the current FSU Facts Sheet for current rates.
- In RAMP, fringe-benefit budgeting also includes health-insurance considerations.
- The page identifies the OMNI HR query `FSU_SRAS_RAMP_FRINGE_BE_CALC` for determining the FB percentage, or it may be calculated manually.
- Manual method: calculate applicable fringe plus applicable health insurance, then divide that total by applicable salary.
- Page example: applicable salary $16,500; fringe at about 21.476% = $3,540.90; plus $2,304 health insurance = $6,844.90 total FB; $6,844.90 / $16,500 = 41.48% FB.
- Health insurance depends on individual, spouse, or family coverage and should be prorated according to project effort/person-months.
- If a 9-month faculty appointment requests only summer months, health insurance should not be budgeted.

### Equipment

- Equipment is an item costing more than **$5,000** with a useful life greater than one year.
- Equipment costs are excluded from the MTDC F&A base.

### Travel

- Necessary project travel for conferences, workshops, or research meetings is generally allowable as a direct cost.
- Costs may include airfare/mileage, lodging, ground transportation, registration, and per diem.
- Separate domestic from foreign/international travel.
- Budgeted travel must follow FSU travel policies.

### Other Direct Costs

#### Tuition

- Graduate students paid by the project must have tuition budgeted or covered by another source under FSU policy.
- Consult the current FSU Facts Sheet for rates.
- Tuition is excluded from both TDC and MTDC F&A bases.

#### Materials and Supplies

- Project-specific supplies are generally allowable direct costs.
- General-purpose office supplies such as paper, toner, pens, and staples are normally indirect and should not be directly budgeted unless justified.
- Itemize materials/supplies and provide quantities and approximate unit costs.

#### Publication Costs

Publication costs associated with disseminating research results in professional journals are generally allowable direct costs.

#### Consultant Services

- A consultant is generally an expert adviser paid a fixed rate for time/services.
- Consultant costs can be allowable direct costs when they directly benefit the project.
- If the consultant works for another university, confirm compliance with that institution's consulting policy. Employees of other institutions generally should be budgeted as subawards.

#### Subawards and Contractors

- When FSU passes federal funds to another organization, FSU must determine whether the relationship is a subaward or contractor/vendor relationship.
- Use the Subaward or Contractor/Vendor Determination Guide.
- The page states that the portion of each subaward over $25,000 is excluded from the MTDC F&A base.
- In a RAMP proposal budget, add subawards using the **Create Subaward** activity rather than including them directly in the FSU budget. RAMP combines the FSU and subaward budgets for the total proposal amount.

#### Rent

- Rent is generally allowable when project work occurs in space not owned by FSU.
- Rent is excluded from the MTDC F&A base and generally signals use of an off-campus F&A rate.

#### Other

Other costs may be direct costs when they are allowable, allocable, reasonable, and consistently treated.

### Participant Support Costs

- Participants are generally recipients of training or services at workshops, conferences, or other short-term instructional/information-sharing activities.
- Participant travel, subsistence, stipends (not employee stipends), and tuition for non-payroll trainees on training grants may be budgeted.
- Participant support costs are excluded from the MTDC F&A base.
- Consult FSU's Participant Support Costs policy.

### Facilities and Administrative (F&A) Costs

- F&A recovery covers part of FSU's institutional costs of supporting faculty research.
- Applicable rates/bases depend on sponsor, location, and project purpose; use the current FSU Facts Sheet for current rates.
- FSU uses Total Direct Costs (TDC) and Modified Total Direct Costs (MTDC) bases.
- Tuition is excluded from TDC.
- MTDC exclusions listed on the page include equipment, capital expenditures, patient-care charges, student tuition remission, rental costs of off-site facilities, scholarships/fellowships, participant support costs, and the portion of each subaward exceeding $25,000.
- The page's worked example uses $136,000 direct costs. MTDC exclusions of $25,000 produce a $111,000 base; at a 54% example rate, F&A is $59,940. TDC exclusions of $10,000 produce a $126,000 base; at a 10% example rate, F&A is $12,600.
- The page says to round budget costs to the nearest dollar.
- Consult FSU Direct/Indirect Costs and Indirect Cost Recovery policies and the Research Administration Manual (RAM) for additional guidance and a sample budget justification.
- The page also points to the Building Budgets module (ESP5) in the eSPEAR Certificate Series in Canvas.

## CAS Exemptions

A project may exceptionally treat costs normally considered indirect as direct costs when it meets the definition of exceptional circumstances and the costs are specifically identifiable to that sponsored project, reasonable, allowable, and allocable.

Possible costs include general-purpose office supplies/software, postage, and local telephone costs.

### Exceptional circumstances listed by FSU

- Large, complex program.
- Extensive data accumulation, analysis, and entry.
- Travel-arrangement work for large numbers of participants.
- A principal focus on preparing/producing manuals, large reports, or books.
- A project location geographically inaccessible to normal administrative services.

## Cost Sharing

- Cost sharing/matching is the share of project costs not paid by the sponsor.
- "Project enhancement" describes available resources without quantifying them in the proposal and is not treated as a cost-sharing commitment.
- FSU's policy is to cost share only when required by the sponsor or the competitive nature of the award.
- Budget only the amount needed to satisfy sponsor requirements.
- Exceptions require chair and dean approval.

### Required proposal-stage documentation

- Cost Share Commitment Form for university contributions.
- Third Party Commitment Form for non-university contributions.
- RAMP cost-share budget and justification.

Cost-shared costs must meet the same requirements as sponsor-requested costs. For federally funded and federal flow-through projects, costs must comply with 2 CFR 200 and CAS.

### University contributions

Possible forms include:

- Unrecovered F&A when the sponsor mandates a rate below FSU's negotiated rate.
- Salaries and fringe benefits paid from non-sponsored accounts and documented through FACET.
- Other direct costs paid from non-sponsored accounts.

The page notes this list is not exhaustive and advises contacting SRA about other contemplated cost-share commitments.

### Non-university contributions / third-party in-kind

Possible contributions include:

- Property, equipment, supplies, and similar contributions.
- Goods and services directly benefiting the project.

Third parties named in a proposal must submit a commitment form at proposal stage and periodically provide documentation during the award period supporting their contribution.

## Subrecipient/Contractor Requirements

FSU must determine whether a person/entity providing services under an FSU grant or contract should be treated as a contractor/vendor or subrecipient.

### Contractor / Vendor

- Provides a good or service routinely supplied to the general public and not unique to the project.
- Typically a for-profit entity and generally not subject to federal compliance requirements described for subrecipients.
- FSU usually issues a purchase order through the Purchasing Office.
- The page states FSU collects F&A on the total amount budgeted for a contractor.

### Subrecipient

- Performs activities with a significant impact on the overall project and a distinct deliverable unique to the project, generally with minimal PI supervision.
- May perform an important part of the federal program and may sometimes function as a co-PI or project partner.
- FSU issues a subaward through SRA.
- The page states FSU collects F&A only on the first $25,000 budgeted for each subaward.

### Proposal-stage contractor documents

- Subrecipient/Contractor Determination Guide.
- Quote or other documentation confirming the budgeted amount when a specific contractor is named.

### Proposal-stage subrecipient documents

- Subrecipient/Contractor Determination Guide.
- Letter of Intent signed by the subrecipient's authorized organizational official.
- Subrecipient-specific scope of work.
- Subrecipient budget and budget justification, preferably in the sponsor-required format.
- Other sponsor-required documents, such as biographical sketches and current/pending support.

## Submitting Your Proposal

- If an electronic portal requires approval/submission by FSU's authorized official, SRA submits the proposal.
- The PI may submit proposals directly by email, mail, or hand delivery only after receiving SRA approval.
- The page links to the myFSU Portal/RAMP, FastLane at FSU (NSF), and FSU Electronic Research Administration resources.

## AI-Agent Guardrails

When this file is used as agent knowledge:

1. Treat it as FSU-specific proposal-development guidance, not universal grant policy.
2. Do not infer that an expense, proposal, or sponsor is approved merely because it fits a category above.
3. For current fringe, tuition, F&A, or other changing rates, direct the user to the current FSU Facts Sheet rather than relying on examples in this file.
4. For federal proposals, check the current version of 2 CFR 200 and sponsor-specific solicitation requirements.
5. Treat limited-submission listings as non-exhaustive; if an opportunity is absent, direct the user to ORD.
6. Preserve the three-working-day SRA routing requirement and distinguish it from the sponsor's own deadline.
7. When classification of a subrecipient versus contractor is uncertain, use FSU's determination process rather than guessing.
8. When a proposed cost appears exceptional, unusual, or normally indirect, route the question to SRA and applicable FSU/CAS guidance.
